PLAY 
FUNCTIONS OF PLAY 
❑ Educational 
❑ Recreational 
❑ Physical Development 
❑ Social and Emotional Adjustment; learn moral 
values; develop the idea of sharing 
❑ Therapeutic 
 
TYPES OF PLAY 
• OBSERVATION/SOLITARY/ONLOOKER 
– 
Watches particular play intently although not 
engaged in it. 
• PARRALEL 
– 
Two children play side by side but seldom interact 
with each other 
• ASSOCIATIVE 
– 
Children play together in a similar activity with 
little organization of responsibilities. 
• COOPERATIVE 
– 
Children play with an organized structure or 
compete for desired goal or outcome 
 
 
CRITERIA FOR JUDGING THE  
SUITABILITY OF TOYS 
 
• Safety 
 
• Compatibility: child’s age; level of development; 
experience 
 
• Usefulness 
✓ enhance social and personality dev. 
✓ increase motor and sensory skills 
✓ develop creativity 
✓ implement therapeutic procedures 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
PLAYING DURING TODDLERHOOD  
(PARALLEL PLAY) 
• The child plays alongside other children but not with 
them 
• Mostly free and spontaneous, no rule or regulations 
• Attention span is still very short, and change of toys 
occurs at frequent intervals 
• Safety is important; there is danger of: 
✓ Breaking a toy through exploration and ingesting 
small pieces 
✓ Ingesting lead from lead-based paint on toys 
✓ Being burned by potentially flammable toys 
• Imitation and make-believe play begins by end of the 
second year 
• Suggested toys 
✓ Play furniture, dishes, cooking utensils, telephone 
✓ Puzzles with a few large pieces 
✓ Pedal - propelled toys, such as tricycle; straddle 
toys such as rocking horse 
✓ Clay, sandbox toys, crayons, finger paints 
✓ Pounding toys; toys; blocks; push-pull toys 
 
PLAY DURING PRESCHOOL YEARS  
(ASSOCIATIVE PLAY) 
• Through play, the child deals with reality, learns control 
of feelings, and expresses emotions more through action 
than through words 
• Play is still physically oriented but is also imitative and 
imaginary 
• Increasing sharing and cooperation among preschool 
children, especially 5-year-old children 
• Suggested toys 
✓ Dress-up clothes; dolls; house; small trucks; 
animals. puppets; etc. 
✓ Painting sets, coloring books, paste, and cut-out 
sets 
✓ Illustrated books, puzzles with large pieces and 
more shapes 
✓ Tricycles wing; slide; other playground equipment 
 
PLAY DURING SCHOOL-AGED YEARS 
 
• Play activities vary with age; number of play activities 
decreases, whereas the amount of time spent in one 
particular activity increases 
• Likes games with rules because of increased mental 
abilities 
• Likes games of athletic competition because of increased 
motor ability 
• Should learn how to work as well as play, with a 
beginning appreciation for economics and finances 
• In beginning of school years, boys and girls play together 
but gradually separate into sex-oriented type of activities

PAIN ASSESSMENT 
 
TODDLER 
❑ Generalized restlessness; guards or rubs painful 
area 
❑ Loud crying: uses words to describe pain (e.g., 
BooBoo, ouch) 
❑ Tries to delay painful situations 
 
PRESCHOOLER 
❑ Crying, can describe pain location 
❑ Regression to earlier stage of development; 
withdrawal 
❑ May believe pain is punishment for bad behavior 
❑ May have been told to be brave and deny pain; 
fear of injections may contribute to denial of pain 
❑ May hit or kick caregiver 
 
SCHOOL AGE 
❑ Stiff body posture; withdrawal 
❑ Able to describe pain 
❑ Afraid of bodily harm; may delay or bargain to 
avoid painful situations 
❑ Recognizes that death exists 
 
ADOLESCENT 
❑ Increased muscle tension; decreased activity; 
withdrawal  
❑ Describes location and intensity of pain 
❑ Perceives pain at physical, emotional, and mental 
levels
