ERIKSON’S THEORY OF PSYCHOSOCIAL 
DEVELOPMENT 
 
I. INFANT (TRUST VS MISTRUST)  
– 
are learning confidence and love, if not met 
become fearful suspicious of the world and people 
 
 
 
• Infant’s basic needs are either met or not met by the 
parents 
• Parents are expected to provide food, caring, comfort 
• Infants learn whether or not people are reliable 
 
II. TODDLER (AUTONOMY VS SHAME AND DOUBT)  
– 
become more independent and make decisions for 
himself.  
– 
when caregivers are impatient, they enforce 
shame and doubt. 
 
III. PRESCHOOLER (INITIATIVE VS GUILT)  
– 
Initiative is encouraged when parents answer 
their child’s question. 
– 
Those who do not develop may later have limited 
brainstorming and problem-solving skills. 
 
IV. SCHOOL AGE (INDUSTRY VS INFERIORITY)  
– 
World grows to include school and community 
environment  
– 
“How to do things well”  
– 
When they are encouraged to do practical tasks – 
praised and rewarded for the finished results, their 
sense of industry grows. 
 
IV. ADOLESCENT (IDENTITY VS. ROLE CONFUSION)  
– 
Learns who he or she is and what kind of a person 
he or she will be by adjusting a new image, 
seeking emancipation from parents, choosing a 
vocation and determining a value system  
– 
 Relate with peers

PIAGET’S THEORY OF COGNITIVE DEVELOPMENT 
 
INFANT (Sensorimotor) 
• Babies relate to the world through senses, using reflex 
behavior 
 
TODDLER (Preoperational Thought) 
• Use of symbol to represent an object  
• Pre logical will lead to faulty conclusions  
• Comprehends simple abstractions but thinking is basically 
concrete and literal  
• Displays static thinking (inability to remember what he 
started to talk)  
• Concept of time – now; Distance – as far as he can see 
 
PRESCHOOLER (Pre-operation thought) 
• Intuitive – children have the tendency to look at 
object and see only one of its characteristics 
(Centering) 
• Thinking is also influenced by role fantasy. 
• Use assimilation (taking information and changing 
it to fit their ideas.) as a part of this. 
• They perceive animals and even inanimate objects as 
being capable of thought and feeling. 
• “magical thinking” 
• Egocentric (unable to see the viewpoint of 
another) 
 
SCHOOL AGE (Concrete Operational)  
• There is an increase logical and coherent method to 
thought process  
 
SYSTEMATIC REASONING  
1. Conservation (Ability to discern truth even though 
physical properties change) 
2. Reversibility (ability to retrace steps) 
 
ADOLESCENT (Formal Operational) 
• Capable of thinking in terms of possibility (abstract 
thought) 
• Can solve hypothetical problems 
• Understand cause and effect

• Child follows rules because of a need to be a good person 
in his own eyes of others  
❑ Level IV – child finds following rules satisfying
